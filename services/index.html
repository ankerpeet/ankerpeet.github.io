01 / Accessibility

Remove barriers before they become blockers.

I go through your most important pages and user flows and check them against the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 at level AA. Most of the work is manual. I use your site the same way people with disabilities do, with a keyboard, a screen reader, and the browser zoomed way in.

You'll get a clear list of what's getting in people's way, who it affects, and how to fix it. The goal is to make accessibility part of how your team builds things, not a last-minute scramble before launch.

What I review

  • Keyboard behavior: logical tab order, no keyboard traps, operable menus, dialogs, and custom widgets.
  • Focus management: visible focus indicators, focus not obscured by sticky UI, and correct focus after route changes and modals.
  • Semantics and structure: landmarks, headings, lists, tables, and accessible names for links, buttons, and controls.
  • Forms: labels, instructions, error identification, error recovery, and redundant-entry requirements.
  • Color and contrast: text, icons, focus rings, and states that rely on color alone.
  • Assistive technology: spot-checks with screen readers and zoom/reflow at 400%.
  • WCAG 2.2 additions: target size, dragging movements, consistent help, and accessible authentication.
02 / Performance

Find what makes the experience feel slow.

I look at your Core Web Vitals (Largest Contentful Paint, Interaction to Next Paint, and Cumulative Layout Shift) using real user data and my own testing.

Sometimes tracking down performance problems feels like searching for a needle in a haystack. I'll find the needle, and I'll rank every fix by how much your users will actually notice it, so your team isn't spending weeks on things nobody will feel.

What I review

  • Core Web Vitals: root causes behind Largest Contentful Paint: how long it takes for the biggest image or block of text on the screen to show up. Good is 2.5 seconds or less., Interaction to Next Paint: how quickly the page visibly responds when someone clicks, taps, or types. Good is 200 milliseconds or less., and Cumulative Layout Shift: how much the content jumps around while the page loads. Good is a score of 0.1 or less., with field data where available.
  • Rendering path: render-blocking resources, critical CSS, server response, and caching.
  • JavaScript: bundle size, long tasks, hydration cost, third-party scripts, and main-thread contention.
  • Images and media: formats, sizing, responsive sources, lazy loading, and LCP image priority.
  • Fonts: loading strategy, subsetting, fallback metrics, and layout shift from font swaps.
  • Network: request waterfalls, preconnects, compression, and CDN usage.
03 / Responsiveness

Make every breakpoint feel intentional.

Most sites look fine at the few screen sizes they were designed for. But real people are using split-screen laptops, big phones turned sideways, tablets, and browsers zoomed in to read more easily.

I test across all of those, including the weird in-between sizes automated tools don't really check, and show you exactly where things break and why.

What I review

  • Navigation: menus, sticky headers, and off-canvas patterns at every size.
  • Forms: input types, mobile keyboards, autofill, and error visibility on small screens.
  • Layouts: overflow, wrapping, grid and flex edge cases, and content reflow.
  • Touch targets: size, spacing, and gesture alternatives.
  • Content behavior: long words, translated strings, tables, media, and embedded content.
  • Orientation and zoom: landscape, text resizing, and browser zoom.
