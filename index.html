One audit. Three dimensions of frontend health.

01

Accessibility

Remove barriers before they become blockers.

I go through your key pages with a keyboard, a screen reader, and the WCAG 2.2 AA guidelines. I look at forms, focus, contrast, and all the little things that can make a site hard (or impossible) for some people to use.

02

Performance

Find what makes the experience feel slow.

I dig into Core Web Vitals, JavaScript, images, and fonts to find what's actually slowing things down. Then I rank the fixes by how much your users will notice them, not just by what's easiest to measure.

03

Responsiveness

Make every breakpoint feel intentional.

I test your navigation, forms, and layouts across real screen sizes, including the awkward in-between ones. Tiny tap targets and layouts that break at certain widths are easy to miss, and automated tools rarely catch them.

A practical guide to Core Web Vitals for busy teams

Largest Contentful Paint: how long it takes for the biggest image or block of text on the screen to show up. Good is 2.5 seconds or less., Interaction to Next Paint: how quickly the page visibly responds when someone clicks, taps, or types. Good is 200 milliseconds or less., and Cumulative Layout Shift: how much the content jumps around while the page loads. Good is a score of 0.1 or less. in plain English, plus the fixes that usually make the biggest difference.
